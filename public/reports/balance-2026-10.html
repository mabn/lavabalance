Short answer

  1. A 50:50 game is close to 50:50, but not quite. The side the bot rated slightly stronger won 57% of "50:50" games, so a typical "50:50" is more like 57:43.
  2. New players are handled correctly now. In September, teams with more new players lost more than they should have. Since the bigger new player handicap went in on Sep 27, those teams win 51% of 50:50 games, exactly as they should.
  3. Players who play a lot are underestimated. In "50:50" games, the team with more lava games in the past week won 59%. When the gap was 20 games or more, it won 61%. This is what looked like a veteran edge: veterans win more because they are the ones playing 20 to 50 games a week, not because of their total experience.
  4. Games are far more even than before the bot. In June to August only 1 game in 20 started close to 50:50, and the favourite won 81% of all games. In October 3 in 4 started close to 50:50, and the favourite won 64%. Most of the remaining lopsided games were played while the balance bot was turned off.

What are lavabalance and LOS?

  • lavabalance is the bot in the Supreme LAVA lobby and its website, lavabalance.fogofwar.dev. Before a game it shows each team's chance of winning. When it is a boss in the lobby, it can balance the teams (!fb) by trying every way to split the 16 players and picking the one closest to 50:50.
  • LOS is a lava-only skill rating. BAR's normal rating says almost nothing about how good someone is at lava, so LOS is built only from LAVA games: 8v8 on Supreme Isthmus, at least 6 minutes long. Winning raises it and losing lowers it, more so when the result was a surprise. The team win chances come from the LOS of everyone on each team.
  • This report covers all 585 LOS-rated games from Sep 28 to Oct 31, compared with earlier months. A game counts as "50:50" when the bot gave it between 45% and 55%.

2. When the bot says 50:50, is it?

Nearly. A "50:50" game plays out at about 57:43. Since Sep 28 the bot called 439 games 50:50. Even then it almost always shows one side slightly ahead, like 50.3% against 49.7%. That side won 249 of the 439 games (57%), while the bot's average for it was 50.5%. Luck alone produces a gap that big about 1 time in 100.

3. Active players: underestimated

To find groups the bot misjudges, take the "50:50" games again and check one kind of team at a time. A group the bot rates correctly should still win about half.

"50:50" games only. "New players" means under 10 lava games. "Games in the past week" counts each player's lava games in the 7 days before the match. Only games where one team had more than the other are counted.

  • Teams with more new players won only 46% of 50:50 games in September. Now they win 51%, which is fair.
  • Teams with more games in the past week won 56% in September (too few games to be sure) and 59% since Sep 28. With 430 games, that is very unlikely to be luck. The bigger the gap, the clearer: under 10 games 49%, 10 to 20 games 59%, 20 to 40 games 63%, over 40 games 60%.

The bot only compares teams, so a team of active players that wins too often means the other team is rated too high. Splitting players by activity and by experience shows where the error sits.

Read down a column: within every experience group, the more someone played that week, the better they did against their rating. Read across a row: once activity is the same, total experience adds little.

  • How big: a player on 20 or more games a week beats their LOS by about 3% per game, a player on fewer than 5 games a week falls about 1 to 2% short. For the team it adds up: a "50:50" game with a 20-game activity gap is really about 61:39.
  • How often: in 2 of every 3 "50:50" games one team had 20 or more games of the past week more than the other. About a quarter of all player slots are filled by players on 20+ games a week.
  • It is the real story behind the veteran edge. Teams with more players of 100+ games won 62% of "50:50" games. But almost all of those veterans play 20 to 50 games a week. Once activity is counted, total experience adds nothing to the prediction, and the "mid-level players overestimated" effect mostly disappears too: mid-level players who play 20+ games a week do better than LOS expects, those who play less do worse.
  • Every week: the more active team came out ahead in each of the five weeks (54%, 56%, 53%, 65%, 66%), and in both halves of October.
  • Parties play only a small part. The team with more players in a party won 53% of "50:50" games. Taking parties into account barely changes the activity edge.
  • It isn't the new player handicap. Which team carries more handicap doesn't predict the result (that team won 54%, within luck). And active players are underestimated in every experience group, including players long past the handicap.
  • When it started: there is no sign of it from January to August. It appears in September, when the bot started balancing, and is clear since Sep 28.

What could be done. LOS treats a player who played 40 games this week and one who played 2 the same way. Two things are worth testing: letting LOS move faster for players who play a lot, or, as the bot already does for new players (section 4), spreading the most active players evenly when several splits are within 2% of each other.

4. The new player handicap

What it is. Every player starts with the same LOS. In practice, players in their first lava games lose more often than that starting rating says. They are still learning the map and the lava tweaks, even if they are good at normal BAR. So for the first 35 lava games, LOS counts a new player as weaker than their raw rating. The discount is biggest in game 1 and shrinks every game until it is gone.

New players start at LOS 3.7. This is how it's calculated:

StepBrand-new playerPlayer on their 10th game, so far average

LOS shown in the lobby3.77.9

The margin shrinks slowly as a player's results come in. In an 8v8 one result tells LOS little about any single player, so it is still about 8 after 10 games. The handicap shrinks much faster, and is gone after game 35. The 10th-game column assumes the player won and lost about evenly so far.

Old handicap (until Sep 27)Current handicap

How much LOS is taken off a new player, by lava game number. A brand-new player shows LOS 3.7 instead of 16.7. The discount is 9 by game 10, 6 by game 20, 3.3 by game 35, and zero from game 36.

  • It is already part of the shown LOS. The number shown in the lobby and on the site includes the discount. It is also what the bot balances with.
  • It doesn't punish new players. Because the bot expects them to be weaker, they lose less LOS for a loss and gain more for a win. Someone who plays well climbs quickly.
  • Why it changed on Sep 27. The old handicap was smaller and stopped after 20 games. Teams with more new players were still losing more than predicted, so it was enlarged and extended to 35 games.
  • New players are also split evenly. When several splits are almost equally balanced (within 2%), the bot picks the one with the most even count of players under 10 games. Players in a party with someone are left out of that count. Now 92% of balanced games have new players split within one of even, up from about 75%.

Did it work? Yes. Teams with more new players went from winning 46% of 50:50 games to 51% (section 3). Re-fitting the handicap from scratch on all games through October lands on the same curve, so it doesn't need another change.

6. Should LOS use BAR OS or chevron? No.

A common suggestion is to start new players from their normal BAR rating (OS) or their chevron instead of the same LOS for everyone. If that would help, players with a high BAR OS would keep winning more than LOS expects, especially in their first games. The charts check exactly that.

Each dot is a group of players: how often their team won, minus the win chance LOS gave it. Zero means LOS judged that group right. Above zero means the group is better than LOS thinks. Pick which of a player's lava games to look at (their 1st to 5th, 6th to 20th, and so on). Whiskers show the range luck alone could explain. All 2026 games, scored with today's ratings. Chevrons are as BAR stores them (0–7).

  • In their first 5 lava games, every BAR OS group plays almost exactly as LOS expects, within about 1% either way. The one exception points the wrong way for the idea: players with BAR OS 30+ did worse than expected, by about 3%.
  • Chevron makes no difference either. Every chevron group lands within about 1% of zero in the first games.
  • Later there is a faint tilt. From about game 6 on, players with high BAR OS (25+) do a little better than LOS expects (about +1 to +3%), and players under 15 a little worse. It is small, and LOS keeps learning from each player's own results. A starting value based on BAR OS would not help here, because these players are already past their start.
  • A month-by-month check agrees. Some months high-OS newcomers did a bit better than LOS expected, and other months a bit worse. A rule built on BAR OS would help one month and hurt the next.
  • Games played is what matters. LOS already treats players in their first 35 lava games as weaker than average (the new player handicap, section 4). With that in place every BAR OS group comes out on target in the first games, which is the only place a BAR-based starting rating could help.
